Quote Intelligence: two builders' quotes, one comparison you can trust

A sales consultant sits beside a customer who asks one question: how does this compare to the other builder's quote? Quote Intelligence answers it with a side-by-side comparison in which every dollar traces back to the line and page it came from, the arithmetic is done by code, and anything the system cannot verify is flagged instead of guessed.

My role

Principal AI Engineer. Orchestration, the no-fabrication design, the deterministic compute engine, MCP tool surface, test suites and production hardening.

In one minute

The AI reasons. Ordinary code guarantees the numbers.

The consultant uploads two quotes, their own and a competitor's. Minutes later they have a side-by-side comparison: every figure traced to the exact line on the exact page, the arithmetic correct to the cent, and every uncertain item flagged for them to resolve.

Reading PDFs is not the hard part; plenty of tools do that. The hard part is trust. A language model left to itself will happily invent a plausible price. So here the model reads, matches and flags, and it never decides what a number is.

  • Every figure shows its receiptNo price reaches the comparison without the page it came from and the exact words on that page.
  • The maths is codeEvery difference, subtotal and headline saving is computed by plain arithmetic, the same kind of code that runs a spreadsheet.
  • Uncertainty is shown, not hiddenAn unverified figure turns amber, the headline total refuses to show a number, and the consultant is told exactly what to resolve.
The problem

A half-day job by hand, and a risky one with a chatbot.

Why it is slow

  • The two quotes arrive in different formats and use different words for the same thing: one says “6.6kW solar system”, the other buries it inside an “energy package”.
  • “Included” items often have no quantity, and promotional discounts expire mid-cycle.
  • The consultant maps the quotes line by line, prices the gaps and lays it out so the customer sees like against like: routinely half a day per customer.

Why generic AI makes it worse

  • Dropping both PDFs into a chatbot fails at exactly the point that matters: the numbers.
  • A model asked to compare quotes will confidently produce a figure that reads right but was never in either document.
  • In a half-million-dollar decision, one fabricated $500 line found by the customer destroys the credibility of the whole comparison, and the sale with it.
The problem, stated precisely: read line items out of two unstructured PDFs, align them across two builders' vocabularies, and reconcile the money to the cent, while making it impossible for the AI to guess a number that is not in the source.
The four ideas

Four ideas explain the whole system.

Everything technical further down is these four, made rigorous.

Idea 1

Specialists, not a generalist

Six agents with one job each: read our quote, read the competitor's, match them up, flag anything ambiguous, double-check every figure, apply edits. Every step can be inspected.

Idea 2

Every number shows its receipt

A figure enters the comparison only with its page and the exact words containing it. A price without a receipt is marked unconfirmed and set aside for a person.

Idea 3

A second opinion from a different model

A different AI model re-reads both PDFs and confirms every figure, the way two independent people are unlikely to misread the same number the same way.

Idea 4

The maths is never left to the AI

Language models are superb at language and unreliable at arithmetic, so the arithmetic is taken away from them and done in exact decimals by code.

The mechanism

A plain-code orchestrator drives six specialists.

The two readers run at the same time. Matching, flagging and the independent double-check run in sequence. Then a pure-code check and the arithmetic engine finish the job.

Why the work is split this way

Reading and matching are language tasks, suited to a fast model. Verification has to be able to disagree with the readers, so it runs on a different model, with a different temperature and prompt. The final check and the arithmetic are plain code; the model layer never touches a sum.

The orchestrator itself is plain Python, not an AI. Every stage streams a progress line to the screen, so a six-minute run never looks frozen.

The three walls

“Every number shows its receipt”, enforced three separate ways.

A single bug in any one wall cannot let a made-up figure through, because the other two still stand. Anything that fails is rewritten to Unconfirmed: amber, no value in the totals, listed for review.

Wall 1evidence or it is flaggedstructural · enforced by the output format

Every priced row must carry a page and a verbatim snippet containing the figure. The readers cannot emit a price without one, and the same rule applies whenever a person or a chat instruction changes a price.

Wall 2an independent second readerdifferent model, temperature and prompt

A different model re-reads the source and confirms each figure. A disagreement is never silently resolved: it marks the row for scrutiny.

Wall 3a literal, mechanical checkplain code · no model involved

The first two walls rely on how the AI is instructed. The third does not use AI at all. It asks one question of every figure: does the quoted snippet appear, word for word, on the page it claims?

# Wall 3, in essence: does the snippet really appear on its cited page?

And the total tells the truthThe arithmetic engine works in exact decimal amounts, with no rounding drift across quotes worth hundreds of thousands. If any row is Unconfirmed, the headline saving blocks at $0 with a note, rather than showing a number built on a figure the system could not stand behind.
What a user actually does

Five steps from the consultant's chair.

Everything in the mechanism happens invisibly between steps 3 and 5.

  1. Enter the basicsProject address, their own name, the competitor's name, the two quote PDFs and the quote dates, which default sensibly. No customer personal details are collected.
  2. Upload the quotesBoth PDFs are read page by page. Scanned, image-only quotes go through an optional vision step; a quote with no readable text gets a clear message, not a silent wrong answer.
  3. Run the comparisonThe six agents go to work while a live panel narrates each step, with a steady heartbeat during the longer stages.
  4. Resolve the flagsProvide a missing quantity, exclude the item, or mark it for discussion. The totals stay blocked until every flag is dealt with.
  5. Review and shareAn editable side-by-side table, a Yes/No inclusions matrix, a plain-language financial position, a Word document to the house template and a colour-coded Excel workbook, all from one data model.

Humans where the money is

The consultant resolves every ambiguity before totals unlock, and only an explicit human action clears an Unconfirmed row, an action that itself has to pass the tripwire. Routine rows need no touch; the system asks only about what the source genuinely does not say.

Production hardening

  • Federated identity; the application never sees a password
  • IDOR guard: a non-owner gets 404, not 403, so session existence is not leaked
  • Zero secrets in code; managed-identity credential chain
  • No customer PII; comparisons keyed by project address; a persistent INTERNAL banner on every export
  • SSRF guard on every outbound fetch; six-layer upload validation
  • Reconnect-safe SSE with sequence numbers; reasoning heartbeats every ~10 s
  • Append-only audit of every field-level edit, seven-year retention

Observability and replay

  • SSE stream reports every agent handoff; reconnect-safe with sequence numbers and reasoning heartbeats
  • Because compute and tripwire are pure code, any recorded comparison replays offline against the current engine
  • Self-verifying suites print the exact assertion that broke and exit non-zero
