Dragonfruit AI Production OS: agents that propose, humans that decide

A YouTube production company runs pods of producers, coordinators, creative directors and editors, and wants each pod to carry seven clients instead of four without hiring in proportion. The answer is not a chatbot. It is sixteen role-specific tools and four role agents wired into the stack the team already uses, with a human gate on every write and a kill switch over the lot.

The problem

Coordination was eating the creative time.

A one-week time audit across 79 people and five roles told the story in percentages. Producers spent 38% of their week on ClickUp administration. Production coordinators spent 40% on the same. Post-production managers ran at 121% utilisation, mostly on Slack and editor management. Editors, the largest group at 54 people, sat idle 6.5% of the time waiting for assignments and spent 13% of their effort on revisions.

The team had already voted with its wallet: 46 editors averaged five AI tools each, seventeen paying out of pocket. What was missing was not enthusiasm but an operating system: something that watched the pipeline, did the coordination, and asked a human before anything client-facing moved.

The constraint that shaped everything: the existing Make and Zapier automations had to stay. Agents extend them; they do not replace them. And the pipeline has to keep working with every agent switched off.

Architecture

Five layers, and the top one is governance.

Event-driven by design: a status change in ClickUp or a comment in Frame.io triggers an agent; the agent reaches systems only through connectors; every write passes a gate.

Governance, safety & guardrails

Every write goes through a gate. Three gates never open on their own.

The pipeline works without agents; agents are additive. That one sentence set the blast radius, and the controls were sized to it.

Seven HITL gates

  • G1 task creation
  • G2 task updates
  • G3 Slack messages
  • G4 client-facing communications · permanent
  • G5 schedule changes
  • G6 financial data · permanent
  • G7 publishing · permanent

Three trust levels

ALWAYS_CONFIRM — the agent proposes, a human approves. The default for every gate.

NOTIFY_ONLY — the agent acts and a human is notified, earned only after 95% or better approval over 50 or more runs.

AUTONOMOUS — read operations only. G4, G6 and G7 can never graduate, whatever the approval rate.

Operational safety

  • Master kill switch: one toggle disables everything
  • Every agent step traced and kept 90 days
  • One-click rollback per automation
  • Each automation launches in one pod first, with rollback toggles
  • Request queuing and caching against API limits
  • Demo mode so development never waits on client API access

How it ships

Six phases, quick wins in the first fortnight, one pod before all pods.

Discovery and quick wins → core operations agents and MCP servers → quality and review → creative intelligence → creative enhancement → pilot pod, then staggered rollout with training, runbooks and handoff.
